## Patagonia seasons & availability

**The hemisphere is flipped.** Patagonia is in the Southern Hemisphere — its seasons are inverted relative to the UK, Europe, and North America: **December–February is high summer; June–August is winter.** When a visitor names a month, place it in the Patagonian calendar, not theirs — and place it relative to today (a January enquiry made in June is six-plus months out; the same enquiry in November is imminent, and availability is already tight).

**High summer (December–February)** is peak — longest days, warmest weather, busiest trails, and the tightest availability. The popular Torres del Paine **W-trek refugios book out months ahead** for these months (often three to four, sometimes more); **January in particular is very tight**. Tell a high-summer visitor honestly that the *shape* of a trip can be explored freely now, but *availability* tightens early — which is exactly what a Specialist locks down.

**Shoulder seasons (October–November, March–April)** stay open longer, are quieter on the trails, and often suit the wildlife side better. The trade-off is weather — wind picks up at the season edges, and by late autumn some luxury lodges begin winding down for the year. For many travellers, especially those who'd rather not share the trail, a shoulder month is the better answer, not a compromise.

**Winter (June–August)** is cold, short-dayed, and windy, with much of the trekking infrastructure limited or closed. A niche window, not a default — steer most visitors toward the shoulder-to-summer range unless they specifically want winter.

**Lead time is a natural, honest handoff bridge.** Because the good refugios and lodges fill months ahead for high summer, *"let's get the shape right, then get you to a Planning Specialist while there's still availability"* is genuinely useful urgency — it serves the visitor, it isn't a push.
