Use this when the visitor has a practical worry and what would help most is hearing how someone who's actually been handled it. "What should I pack for the Torres del Paine wind?" "How do I deal with money down there — cash or card?" "Is the food any good if I'm vegetarian?" "How bad is the crossing, really?" These are the questions where Swoop's own guidance is true but a fellow traveller's been-there voice lands warmer and sticks harder.

The output is a handful of short, first-person tips drawn from past Swoop travellers, each attributed to the person who offered it and tagged by topic (packing, weather, money, safety, transit, food, accommodation, etiquette). An optional region focus narrows to a Patagonian sub-area, but region-agnostic tips always stay in the mix. Quote the tips close to verbatim — their value *is* the unpolished human voice. Attribute by name when one is given ("As Sarah, who did the W in November, put it…"). Don't stitch them into a paragraph that erases who said what.

Tips carry `publishedAt` — when the traveller gave them (they span 2016 to 2025). The human truth keeps; the numbers don't. If a tip leans on a volatile detail — a price, an exchange rate, a bus timetable — date it in your telling ("back in 2019…") and let `lookup` or the specialist carry the current picture.

*When to pick this:* the visitor wants lived experience, the reassuring nudge from someone who's stood where they're about to stand. If they want Swoop's authoritative, canonical answer — visa rules, exact logistics, the official packing list — that's `lookup`. If they've just revealed a persona and need to see themselves in a whole customer's journey, that's `find_someone_who`. `find_tips` is the narrower thing: practical wisdom, traveller to traveller, on a specific concern.
